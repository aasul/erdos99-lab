Play with the dots

Move a dot. Watch what changes. You decide every move.

How to use the playground
  1. Choose a picture below. Each dot is one point.
  2. Drag one dot. Red lines mean two dots are too close.
  3. Try making the furthest pair closer without any red lines.
  4. Save your picture and write down what you noticed.

One unit is our ruler: its size on the screen can change, but its value stays 1. The computer will never fix or rearrange your dots for you.

Advanced: matching allowance

Computers round numbers. With allowance 0.000001, a side from 0.999999 to 1.000001 counts as about 1. This does not relax the red-line rule.

The rule: every pair of dots must stay at least 1 unit apart. Red lines mark distances below 1. A purple triangle has three sides close to 1. The matching allowance above controls how close. A computer picture is evidence to explore, not a mathematical proof.

What do the maths words mean?
Point
A dot. It has a position, but no size.
Distance
The length of the straight line between two dots.
Minimum distance
The distance between the closest pair of dots.
Diameter
The distance between the furthest pair of dots. It is not the width of your screen.
Equilateral triangle
A triangle with three equal sides. Here we look for three sides of length 1.
Configuration
An arrangement or picture of dots.
Conjecture
A mathematical claim that still needs a proof or a disproof.
